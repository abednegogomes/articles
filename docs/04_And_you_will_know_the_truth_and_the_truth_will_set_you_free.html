Regarding "Whitelisted" Sins in the New Testament

In biblical scripture, a strict distinction is drawn between Moral Law (transcendent righteousness grounded in God's holy character) and Ceremonial / Civil Law (Israel's dietary rules, ritual purification rites, and civil temple administration). While ceremonial laws (e.g., Levitical dietary prohibitions or ceremonial feast days) were fulfilled and rendered non-binding in the New Covenant (Acts 10:9–15, Colossians 2:16), zero moral sins are ever whitelisted or sanctioned in the New Testament. Moral transgressions permanently remain prohibited.

Scriptural Analysis: Sins Fulfillment & "Whitelisting"

In biblical theology, no moral sin (such as theft, lying, sexual immorality, murder, or idolatry) is ever "whitelisted" or permitted in the New Testament. In fact, Jesus heightened moral accountability in the Sermon on the Mount by expanding moral standards to internal attitudes (e.g., lust equals adultery; hatred equals murder).

However, Scripture draws a distinction between eternal moral law (reflecting God's character) and ceremonial/civil regulations (given under the Mosaic Covenant as shadows). Several practices that carried severe penalties in the Old Testament as sins of ceremonial disobedience were fulfilled and reclassified as neutral or clean under the New Covenant:

  1. Dietary Laws (Eating "Unclean" Foods)
    • Old Testament: Eating non-kosher foods like pork or shellfish violated ceremonial holiness regulations (Leviticus 11).
    • New Testament Status: Jesus explicitly declared all foods clean, removing ceremonial dietary restrictions.
    • Mark 7:18–19 AMPC: "Thus He was making and declaring all foods [ceremonially] clean [that is, abolishing the ceremonial distinctions of the Levitical Law]."
    • Acts 10:15 AMPC: "What God has cleansed and pronounced clean, do not you call common (unholy or unclean)."
  2. Strict Seventh-Day Sabbath Observance Regulations
    • Old Testament: Doing physical work, traveling beyond designated limits, or gathering firewood on the Sabbath was punishable by death (Exodus 31:14-15).
    • New Testament Status: The strict ceremonial Sabbath rules were set aside as symbolic shadows fulfilled in Christ's spiritual rest.
    • Colossians 2:16–17 AMPC: "Therefore let no one sit in judgment on you in matters of food and drink, or with regard to a feast day or a New Moon or a Sabbath. Such [things] are only the shadow of things that are to come..."
    • Romans 14:5 AMPC: "One man thinks one day is more important than another, while another considers every day alike. Let every man be fully convinced (satisfied) in his own mind."
  3. Ceremonial Ritual Washings & Physical Circumcision
    • Old Testament: Remaining physically uncircumcised or failing to undergo ceremonial bathings after touching dead objects rendered someone cut off from God's community (Genesis 17:14; Numbers 19).
    • New Testament Status: Physical circumcision and ceremonial washings were declared non-essential for covenant standing.
    • Galatians 5:6 AMPC: "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but only faith activated and energized and expressed and working through love."
  4. Mandatory Tithing Regulations
    • Old Testament: Under the Mosaic Law, withholding the required 10% tithes (agricultural produce and livestock for the Levites, temple, and poor) was considered robbing God (Leviticus 27:30; Malachi 3:8-10).
    • New Testament Status: The legalistic 10% obligation attached to the Levitical priesthood was replaced by voluntary, cheerful, and proportional heart-giving.
    • 2 Corinthians 9:7 AMPC: "Let each one [give] as he has made up his own mind and purposed in his heart, not reluctantly or sorrowfully or under compulsion, for God loves (He takes pleasure in, prizes above other things, and is unwilling to abandon or to do without) a cheerful (joyous, 'prompt to do it') giver [whose heart is in his giving]."
    • Hebrews 7:11–12 AMPC: "Now if perfection... was attainable through the Levitical priesthood... what further need was there for another and different Priest to arise... For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is of necessity an alteration of the law [concerning the priesthood] as well."
